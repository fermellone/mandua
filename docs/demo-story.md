# A community garden deciding how to water

A community garden is reviewing how to water its growing beds. The beds do not
all have the same conditions: some get more sun, others more shade, and their
soil moisture varies. The garden's notes say that morning sensor readings are
available before watering.

Two approaches are considered. One uses a fixed morning schedule, with simplicity
as its main attraction. The other uses soil moisture to decide when each bed
needs water, allowing watering to respond to conditions in that bed.

The memory follows the proposals, a recorded choice and a review of that choice.
It also contains an incorrectly transcribed value, a later correction, and a
temporary watering rule that was subsequently withdrawn. Earlier records remain
part of the story even when they no longer describe the current rule.

This is a fictional case created for the Mandu'a proof of concept. It is based on
written observations, proposed rules and recorded decisions, not a real gardening
experiment. The record is intentionally limited: a stated reason or an approval
is not the same as measured evidence that an approach works better.

You can explore the memory as you would ask someone to explain how a small
community arrived at its current practice. The assistant should distinguish what
was recorded, what it infers and what the story does not tell us.
